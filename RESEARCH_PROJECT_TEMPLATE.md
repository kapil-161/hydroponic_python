## Short Description of Your Research

**Introduction with Justification (Scientific/Societal) - <75 words:**

Hydroponic systems offer sustainable food production for urban agriculture and climate-resilient farming. Optimizing these systems requires understanding crop physiology—photosynthesis, respiration, partitioning, transpiration, and water-use efficiency. This research develops a simulation framework integrating 12 physiological models to predict lettuce growth in hydroponic systems. The framework models source-sink relationships, root water uptake, developmental plasticity, and stress responses, enabling precision optimization of environmental conditions for maximum resource-use efficiency and yield.

**Research Question/Hypothesis:**

*How do physiological processes (photosynthesis, respiration, partitioning, transpiration, water uptake) interact to determine lettuce growth and yield in hydroponic systems, and can an integrated simulation framework accurately predict these interactions?*

**Hypothesis:** *The integrated physiological model framework will accurately simulate lettuce growth when parameters are derived from crop physiology data, with prediction accuracy >85% for biomass accumulation, >90% for developmental stage transitions, and >80% for water-use efficiency.*

## Why You Are Participating in the Course and What You Want to Get Out of It

**Why Participating:**
This Crop Physiology course directly addresses the core physiological processes modeled in my simulation framework. The course topics align perfectly with my research needs:

- **Day 1 (Photosynthesis, Respiration, Partitioning):** My framework models Farquhar-von Caemmerer-Berry photosynthesis, maintenance/growth respiration, and functional balance theory for biomass allocation. Lectures by Steven Driever, Ken Boote, and Xinyou Yin will provide essential insights into source-sink relationships and carbon partitioning.

- **Day 2 (Transpiration, Water Uptake, WUE):** My water uptake model uses Penman-Monteith equations and SPAC theory. Lectures by Tino Kreszies, Gerard van der Linden, and Andrea Schnepf will improve my understanding of root water uptake, suberization barriers, and water-use efficiency traits.

- **Day 3 (Development and Plasticity):** My phenology and leaf development models track developmental plasticity and stress responses. Lectures by Jochem Evers, Rumyana Karlova, and Thea van den Berg will enhance my understanding of phenotypic plasticity and root system adaptation.

- **Day 4 (Methodologies):** Data analysis and phenotyping methodologies will help validate my simulation outputs. Lectures on remote sensing and 3D plant models will inform model-data integration.

- **Day 5 (Synthesis):** Gerrit Hoogenboom's lecture on linking crop physiology, modeling, and climate change adaptation directly relates to my framework's application to climate-resilient agriculture.

**What I Want to Get Out of It:**
1. **Physiological Understanding:** Deep knowledge of photosynthesis, respiration, partitioning, transpiration, and water-use efficiency mechanisms to improve model accuracy
2. **Model Validation:** Insights from experimental crop physiology to validate my simulation framework's predictions
3. **Source-Sink Relationships:** Better understanding of carbon allocation and source-sink dynamics (Xinyou Yin's lecture) to refine my biomass allocation model
4. **Water-Use Efficiency:** Knowledge of root traits and above-ground adaptations (Tino Kreszies, Gerard van der Linden) to optimize water-use efficiency predictions
5. **Stress Responses:** Understanding of developmental plasticity and stress mitigation (Rumyana Karlova, Jochem Evers) to improve stress model accuracy
6. **Data Integration:** Methodologies for linking phenotyping data to models (Katarína Smoleňová) to enhance model-data integration
7. **Collaboration:** Connect with crop physiologists and modelers working on similar challenges
8. **Presentation Skills:** Practice presenting my research through poster carousel and group work presentations

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand physiological mechanisms underlying photosynthesis, respiration, and partitioning to validate model equations
- Learn root water uptake mechanisms and water-use efficiency traits to improve transpiration modeling
- Gain insights into developmental plasticity and stress responses to enhance phenology and stress models
- Develop skills in linking experimental data to simulation models
